The Three Sisters.

Gardeners sometimes grow corn, beans, and squash together — each plant different, each with its own job, and the garden does better when they grow together. Your glutes work the same way. The maximus creates powerful hip extension. The medius helps move and control your leg out to the side. The minimus works underneath, keeping your pelvis and thigh steady. You can emphasize one part — you can't ask it to work completely alone.

The Garden Grows Together

Today, some movements will give one part of the garden a little extra attention. Then the final movement will ask the whole garden to grow together.

Today's Movements

You'll use a mini-band standing kickback for the back of the garden, a mini-band lateral walk for the outer garden, and a long-loop hip thrust with a mini band for the whole garden.

You already know each movement. The new challenge is using both bands during the thrust — the long loop asks you to drive your hips up, the mini band asks you to keep your knees gently pressing out. Back, outside, and deep stabilizers all have a job.

If your support can move, place it against a wall before you begin.

  • Sit on the floor with your upper back against your support.
  • Place both feet inside the long loop.
  • Position one side of the loop underneath the middle of both feet.
  • Bring the opposite side of the loop across the crease at the front of your hips.
  • Keep the band flat, centered across your hips, and securely trapped beneath your feet.

The band should now travel: under both feet, alongside your legs, and across the front of your hips.

Check Yourself Against These

This setup is new, so check each of these before your first repetition — not just the first time, every time:

  1. Both feet have entered the loop.
  2. The band sits beneath both feet, not off to one side.
  3. The band crosses the front of your hip crease — flat, not twisted.
  4. Pressing your feet down and pulling gently against the band doesn't shift, roll, or slip it.
Before you lift Wear shoes that let the band stay secure. Don't rest it against a sharp edge or set it up so it can roll off your feet. Press both feet firmly into the floor and gently pull against the band before your first rep — if it shifts, rolls, or feels like it could slip, stop and reset it.
To exit safely Lower your hips back to the floor first — this slackens the band. Then slide one foot out of the loop at a time. Never stand up while your feet are still inside a stretched band.
  • Set your feet about hip-width apart.
  • Press your knees gently outward until they line up with the middle of your feet.
  • You don't need to force them as wide as possible — just enough to feel the mini band.
  • Hold that outward press for the whole repetition, not just at the bottom.

Your knees are elevator doors. Don't let them close while your hips ride up.

Practice 5 slow repetitions. If one band changes the movement so much that you can't reach the top, use the lighter version of that band.

Set a timer for five minutes. Move continuously through the sequence below. This one should feel physically tough — it shouldn't feel frantic.

1. Mini-Band Standing Kickback10 reps right, 10 reps left

Close the door behind you with your heel. Keep the imaginary drink on your head.

If this is too difficult, try
  • A lighter mini band.
  • A smaller kick without leaning.
If this is too easy
  • Keep the same band and reps — today's growth lesson lives in the two-band thrust below.

2. Mini-Band Lateral Walk10 steps right, 10 steps left

Stay on two train tracks. Don't let them crash into each other.

If this is too difficult, try
  • A lighter mini band.
  • Smaller steps with the band above your knees.
If this is too easy
  • Keep the same band and steps — today's growth lesson lives in the two-band thrust below.

The timer measures your workout — not how quickly you can wrestle with your bands. Take the time you need to enter and exit the long loop safely. Completely exit the long loop before standing.

3. Two-Band Hip Thrust10 reps

Press your feet into the floor. Drive your hips into the long loop. Keep gentle outward pressure against the mini band. Pause at the top without arching your back.

Ride the elevator up without letting the knee doors close.

If this is too difficult, try
  • A lighter mini band.
  • Removing the mini band and keeping the long loop — that's today's regression, not a failure.
If this is too easy
  • Keep both bands and today's 10 reps. Using both together already is this lesson's progression — the next one comes in a future lesson.

Continue for Five Minutes

Return to the kickbacks and repeat. Move with enough control that you can still tell which job you're asking your garden to do — the last few repetitions should be slow, not sloppy.

Wrap-up

How Did Your Garden Grow?

The kickback placed extra attention on the back of the garden. The lateral walk placed extra attention on the outer garden. The two-band thrust asked the entire garden to cooperate.

That's how your glutes work outside Booty Garden too. Sometimes one part leads. The others still help.

Today's Way to Grow: Combine Resistance

Adding the mini band to the long-loop thrust is today's only progression. Use both when you can complete your regular long-loop thrust with full height, a quiet rib cage, and at least 3 good repetitions still available.

If adding the mini band makes your thrust shorter or sends the work into your lower back, remove it. You haven't failed the progression — you've found the version your whole garden can use.

What You Know Now

You've met all three parts of your garden. The gluteus maximus helps create size and power through hip extension. The gluteus medius helps shape the outer hip while creating and controlling movement to the side. The gluteus minimus works beneath it, helping stabilize your pelvis and control your thigh.

They have different emphases. They share the work. As Booty Garden continues, you'll see these jobs again and again — extend, move outward, stabilize. Sometimes one lesson will emphasize a single job. Sometimes one exercise will ask the entire garden to work at once.

Now you know how your garden grows.
