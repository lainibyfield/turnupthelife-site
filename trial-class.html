Allow up to 70 minutes while you learn the movements. It gets faster once the order is familiar — the workout doesn't change, your hunting-for-what's-next time disappears. Don't rush the first one to hit a clock.

Get low. Build. Rise.

Pilates-informed strength with two 10 lb dumbbells — so you can wine low, jump the road, and wave your flag till the last truck passes. The workout is right below. Press play, tick things off, go.

Built for the road, whichever one is yours — Trinidad · Miami · Crop Over · Caribana · Notting Hill · Labor Day. Whenever your carnival lands, start now.

Fellas — yes, you too. Same workout, same rules. If 10s truly feel light, start with 20s or 25s and follow every pause and slow count. Then come talk to us.

Brand new to exercise? Start with 10 lb or under — even 5s, even a full water bottle in each hand. Holding on is grip work, and grip counts. Master the movement first; the pounds can wait.

All you need for this lesson 2 dumbbells (≈10 lb) a sturdy chair That's the whole list — everything else below has a swap built in.

Why this matters after 40: around menopause, women lose muscle faster — and muscle isn't just strength, it's how you stay healthy, mobile, and independent for decades. The good news the research is clear on: resistance training rebuilds it, even in your 50s and beyond. This is that training, sized for your living room. See the science ↓

Optional extras & swaps

Nice to have, never required: a 15 lb kettlebell (swap: one dumbbell), a box or bench (swap: your chair, a bottom stair, or a very sturdy toilet lid), an ab wheel (swap: slow plank walkouts), a resistance band (swap: slow shoulder circles).

Weight range: 10s are home base — go lighter (5s or water bottles) if you're brand new, heavier (20–25s, fellas) if you're sure. The equipment isn't the workout — the control is.

The two labels you'll see (Pace & Effort)

Pace — "5 down · 2 hold": seconds to lower, then seconds to hold at the hardest point. Slow is how a 10 turns into a 20.

Effort — "1–2 left": stop when you've got one or two good reps left in the tank — not when it gets annoying. "Empty the tank" means go till the next clean rep won't happen.

The five house cues (read once, hear them forever)

Ribs down — don’t let them pop forward when the weight gets heavy. Like you’re keeping someone from stealing your heart. Laugh and you can feel it.

Heavy shoulders — your neck isn’t invited to this exercise. Grow taller — length first, then strength. Own the movement — don’t survive it. And if you lose control, you’ve already gone too heavy.

Why I built this (the real story)

I'm a data person — I literally kept a spreadsheet of my lifts. It got exhausting to track, and heavier weights were their own hassle. So I built something light enough that you don't need a spreadsheet: slow counts and honest effort do the work instead.

Then my own health data showed me something that changed the mission. Years of Pilates had built me real strength — but post-menopause, I was losing muscle anyway. The science is blunt about it: staying healthy as we age means keeping the muscle, not just the strength. That's why this exists. (My son also swears 10s are too light. He's wrong, and this proves it.)

Move Prep

5–7 min

This is where your wine lives — low, loose, and in control, without your knees or back paying for it later.

Before you begin

Today's warm-up prepares your body for today's workout — not for a flexibility contest.

  • Work only in a pain-free range.
  • Hold a chair, wall, or countertop anytime balance limits the movement.
  • Smaller movements done well beat bigger movements done poorly.
  • If something feels sharp, unstable, or wrong, reduce the range or skip that exercise and move on.

Core Activation

3–5 min

Wake the deep core first — everything that follows leans on it. Not about a six-pack; about the inner corset.

Timer

Dead Bugdeep core — the statue drill

Freeze everything from your shoulders to your hips like a statue. Then move only your arms and legs.

Sets 2Reps 10 / sideWeight bodyweight

Set up first — this is most of the exercise
Set your shoulders before anything else. Pull your shoulder blades down and together, like you're juicing a lemon between them. Pull your belly button toward your spine and try to close the gap under your low back — without letting your butt lift.

  • Hold that setup. From your shoulders to your hips, you are a statue.
  • Now move the arms and legs together: the left arm reaches back overhead as the left knee draws in toward your chest.
  • At the same time the right arm reaches forward toward your hip as the right leg extends long.
  • Then switch sides, slow and even.

Too difficult?
Keep your arms pointed at the ceiling instead of reaching overhead. Your torso still stays the statue — only the legs move.

Feel: deep belly, ribs pinned down.   Not: back arching or ribs popping up — if that happens, shorten the movement.

Legs

12 min
Timer

A1Bulgarian Split Squatquad focus — back foot up on a box, bench, or chair

Pace 5 down · 2 hold Effort empty the tank Reps 8–12 / leg Weight 1 DB or KB at chest

Imagine your zipper stays vertical.

  • Back foot on the box, bench, chair, or step; weight held at your chest.
  • Stay more upright than you think; let the front knee travel forward over the toes.
  • Drive through the middle of the foot — think about straightening the front knee, not pushing your hips back.
  • Let the back leg help only enough to keep your balance.
  • Five seconds down, pause 2 near the bottom, stand up with control.
  • Then set the weight down and keep going bodyweight until your form starts to slip.
Pilates note
Today we’re making this a quad exercise. The RDLs that follow shift the emphasis to the back of the legs.

A2Deficit Single-Leg RDLstanding on the edge of a box for extra reach

Pace 3 down · 3 hold Effort 1–2 left Reps 10–15 / side Weight 2 × 10 DB 🌱 booty fertilizer

Close the car door with your butt.

  • Stand near the edge of the box so the dumbbells can travel below your foot.
  • Hinge from the hips — soft bend in the standing knee, long spine.
  • Reach the hips back.
  • Pause in the stretch 2–3 seconds — stop before your back rounds.
  • Want more? Hold both dumbbells in the hand opposite the working leg.
Easy does it
Don't chase depth if your toe, knee, or hip starts changing the movement. Match reps and range to the stiffer side.
Why this matters

This loads the hamstrings and glutes where they’re longest — one of the best places to build strength and shape.

Personal observation
Your anatomy is your own, but for me, training my inner thighs made a visible difference in the shape of my legs. This is Pilates-informed hypertrophy training — not basic or traditional strength training.

Push

10 min
Timer

Push-ups, Three Waysgo until you can't, then make them easier — twice

Pace 4 down · 1 hover · pop up Effort empty the tank Rounds 2–3 Rest 90 sec

Screw your hands into the floor. Pull yourself to the floor. Push the floor away.

  • Four seconds down, hover 1 second off the floor, then pop up.
  • Full push-ups until you can't keep them clean.
  • Straight into push-ups from your knees.
  • Finish holding halfway down as long as you can.
Why this matters

Chest and shoulders are obvious. The surprise is how much your core has to work when you refuse to let your ribs sag.

Pull

10 min
Timer

C1One-arm Row Drop Setfull reps, then partials, then a hold — same weight throughout

Pace 5 · 2 · 2 · 2 Effort to failure Weight 15 KB or 1 × 10 DB

Squeeze your shoulder blades together like you’re squeezing the juice out of a rolled mango. (Or a lemon, if you’ve never rolled a mango.)

  • Full reps — 10–12 to technical failure. Pace 5-2-2-2: 5 seconds lowering, 2-second stretch at the bottom, 2 seconds pulling, 2-second squeeze at the top.
  • No kettlebell? Use one 10 lb dumbbell with the same tempo. If you still aren't close to failure, grow the squeeze at the top to 3 seconds before you add reps.
  • Straight into bottom-half partials — 10 slow reps. Start with your arm almost straight, where the lat is at its longest. Pull only until your elbow is about halfway back, then lower again — stay in that stretched bottom half. watch
  • Straight into the top hold. Elbow tight to your side, shoulder blade squeezed toward your back pocket. Hold 20–30 seconds.
  • Switch sides and repeat.
Why this matters

A strong upper back helps you stand taller and keeps your shoulders from doing work your back should be doing.

Shoulders

5–7 min
Timer

One-Arm Lateral Raise Ladderone arm at a time — little dumbbells, big burn

Rounds 2–3 Effort 1 left Rest 60–75 sec

Lead with your elbows. Heavy shoulders.

  • 10 half raises.
  • As many full raises as you can with good form.
  • Hold 10 seconds at the highest position you can keep with good form.
  • Repeat on the other arm.
Why this matters

Strong shoulders create width; a strong upper back creates posture. Together they build what most people call a “V-taper” — or, more simply, a strong-looking back.

Why light-but-hard works — the research

The muscle you keep keeps you.

Short version: after menopause women lose muscle faster, muscle loss (sarcopenia) is what steals independence with age, and resistance training is the proven way to fight back — you can build muscle in your 50s and beyond.

  • Around menopause, falling estrogen accelerates the loss of muscle mass and strength — women lose muscle faster than same-age men. Geraci et al., review — Estrogen deficiency & sarcopenia (PMC8934617)
  • Sarcopenia — the gradual loss of muscle mass and strength — drives falls, fractures, and loss of independence. Keeping muscle isn't vanity; it's how you stay mobile for decades. Maturitas — Menopause & sarcopenia
  • It's reversible. Progressive resistance training measurably increased muscle mass in postmenopausal women — one study saw a significant rise in quadriceps cross-sectional area after just 9 weeks. Tracy et al., via PMC8934617 review
  • The winning dose is doable at home: resistance training about 3 sessions a week, 20–90 minutes, for 6+ weeks, is the most effective non-drug way to protect muscle in menopausal women. Systematic review & meta-analysis — Non-pharmacological interventions (PMC10647115)

Shared for education, not medical advice. These are general research findings, not a treatment plan for any individual — talk to your own clinician about what's right for you, especially around menopause and hormone therapy.
