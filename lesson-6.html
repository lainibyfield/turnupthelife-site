Lesson 06 · Travel Workout 2

Pack Light,
Train Hard.

Until now you've created tension with your own body as the anchor. Today you add one fixed anchor point and use it to press, pull, and resist rotation — without adding more equipment.

Same band. New anchor skill.

PrincipleOne anchor. A complete workout.
RoleBalanced full body, with anchored push, pull, and anti-rotation work.
TimeAbout 45–60 minutes.
EquipmentLight, medium, and heavy long bands or tubes, plus a door anchor or other secure anchor at chest height.
MoodTravel · one setup · three directions.

First time through
Allow up to 70 minutes while you learn the movements. It gets faster once the order is familiar — the workout doesn't change, your hunting-for-what's-next time disappears. Don't rush the first one to hit a clock.

Need the band rules? Tap here

Which band for what:

  • Light band — shoulders, pull-aparts, lateral raises.
  • Medium band — chest press, overhead press, face pulls.
  • Heavy band — squats, rows, good mornings, glute bridges.
  • A set only counts if the last few reps are hard and clean.
  • 25 easy reps = the band is too light or too slack. Step wider, shorten the band, take out slack.

Equipment note. This workout assumes long bands or tubes. Packed only mini-loops? The squats, rows, presses, and good mornings need a swap — the anchor lines won't work as written.

How travel week works

Two workouts, each done twice. Not four new workouts.

Day 1Create Tension
Day 2Rest — walk, swim, shop, enjoy the trip
Day 3Pack Light, Train Hard
Day 4Rest — fun movement
Day 5Create Tension — progressed
Day 6Rest — fun movement
Day 7Pack Light, Train Hard — progressed

"Progressed" never means new exercises. Pick one per exercise: next heavier band · shorten the band · add a 2-second squeeze · add a 3-second return · keep tension between reps · add 2–5 reps · shorten transitions only if form stays clean.

Both workouts once ≈ 10 hard sets per major muscle group. Both twice ≈ 20. That's enough. Don't turn your trip into punishment.

Anchor Rulesread first

Until now your feet, back, and hands were the anchor. Today you will attach the band at chest height and leave it there for several exercises.

  1. Use a real anchor. A door anchor is best. A heavy object is acceptable only if it cannot slide, tip, or open.
  2. Anchor on the closing side of the door. Pulling should make the door more secure, not pull it open.
  3. Test it standing first. Give the band several firm, controlled pulls before putting your body in its path.
  4. Chest height is today's default. Set the anchor near the middle of your breastbone.
  5. Change difficulty with distance. Step farther away for more tension; step closer for less.
  6. Finish every rep with control. The band should never pull you toward the door.

Use the band you actually own
No mini-band? Tie a long band into a loop — that's all a mini-band is. Only got a thick, heavy band? Take a shorter grip, or step closer to whatever it's anchored to, until the starting tension feels moderate. The band you have is the right band; adjust the length, not the exercise.

Set it once
Pallof press, chest press, and row all use the same chest-height anchor. You will change which direction you face — not keep rebuilding the setup.

Before you begin

Today's warm-up prepares your body for today's workout — not for a flexibility contest.

  • Work only in a pain-free range.
  • Hold a chair, wall, or countertop anytime balance limits the movement.
  • Smaller movements done well beat bigger movements done poorly.
  • If something feels sharp, unstable, or wrong, reduce the range or skip that exercise and move on.

Standing Pallof Pressanti-rotation — the anchor tries to turn you

The band pulls sideways. You stay square.

You already know this one
You've practised resisting rotation every time you carried something heavy in one hand and stayed tall instead of leaning. This is the same skill from a fixed anchor — the band tries to twist you instead of a bag pulling you over. Your job isn't to move. It's to stay square.

Sets 2–3Reps 8–12 / sideBand light or mediumPace press · 2 hold · return

Anchor / setupAnchor the band at chest height. Stand sideways to it and hold the band at the centre of your chest.

  • Stand sideways to the anchor — one shoulder facing it.
  • Hold the handle with both hands at the centre of your chest.
  • Step away until the band has moderate tension; stack ribs over hips, soften the knees.
  • Press straight out from your sternum. Stay facing forward the whole time.
  • Bring it back with control. Don't let the band rotate you.
  • Complete all the reps, then turn around and repeat with the anchor on the other side.

Feel: the front and sides of the trunk resisting rotation.   Not: twisting through the back or shrugging.

Do thisStand sideways to the chest-height anchor, both hands centred on the handle. Press straight out, hold 2 seconds, return. 8–12 per side.If that’s too difficultStep closer to the anchor and shorten the press.If that’s too easyStep farther away or hold the fully extended position for 4 seconds.
Watch the form

Push

8–10 min

Until today, your upper back anchored the press. Now the band is fixed behind you, so your stance and trunk must control the return.

Timer

B1Anchored Band Chest Presschest-height anchor — press away from it

Ribs down. Press straight forward.

Sets 3Reps 10–20Band mediumPace 3 return · press strongEffort 1–2 good reps left

Anchor / setupAnchor the band at chest height behind you. Face away from the anchor with one end in each hand. Use a staggered stance.

  • Step forward until the band has moderate starting tension.
  • Keep hands and elbows slightly below shoulder height.
  • Press straight forward without flaring the ribs.
  • Return until the hands reach the sides of the chest.
  • Do not let the band pull you backward.

Feel: chest and front shoulder.   Not: pinching at the top of the shoulder.

Do thisFace away from the chest-height anchor in a staggered stance. Press straight forward for 10–20 reps.If that’s too difficultStep closer to the anchor.If that’s too easyStep farther forward, or take 4 seconds to return.
Watch the form

B2Band Push-Upself-anchored — the band is on your back, not the door

Clean reps beat dramatic reps.

Sets 2Reps 8–15Band light or medium

Anchor / setupPush-up: band across the upper back, ends pinned under both palms. Press-out: no anchor — hold one end in each hand at your chest and press straight out.

  • If doing band push-ups, the band goes across the upper back.
  • If that’s too much, do standing band press-outs instead.
  • Screw your hands into the floor. Push the floor away.
  • Stop before the shoulders or wrists complain.

Wrist note
Fists or push-up handles if the wrists object. Press-outs skip the wrist load entirely.

Do thisBand across your upper back, push-ups from the knees or the floor. 8–15 reps.If that’s too difficultDrop the band and put your hands on a chair or countertop.If that’s too easyFeet elevated, or slow the lowering to 3 seconds.

Pull

8–10 min

Until today, your feet anchored the row. Now you face a fixed anchor and control your whole body against its pull.

Timer

C1Anchored Band Rowsame anchor — now you face it

Pull your elbows beside your ribs.

Sets 3Reps 10–20Band medium or heavyPace 2 pull · 2 squeeze · 3 returnEffort 1–2 good reps left

Anchor / setupLeave the band anchored at chest height. Face the anchor, hold one end in each hand, and step back into moderate tension.

  • Stand tall, or use a small hip hinge.
  • Begin with arms extended and shoulders heavy.
  • Pull the elbows back beside the ribs.
  • Finish with the hands near the sides of the chest.
  • Return slowly without being pulled forward.

Feel: mid-back and lats.   Not: shoulders shrugging up toward the ears.

Do thisFace the chest-height anchor and row for 10–20 controlled reps.If that’s too difficultStep closer to the anchor.If that’s too easyStep farther back, or hold the squeeze for 2 seconds.

One setup change
The first three anchored exercises used chest height. For face pulls, move the anchor to face height.

C2Anchored Band Face Pullone deliberate anchor-height change

Hands beside the ears. Neck stays out of it.

Sets 2Reps 12–20Band light or mediumPace 2 pull · 2 squeeze · 3 return

Anchor / setupMove the anchor from chest height to face height. Face it and step back until the band begins with light tension.

  • Pull toward your face with the elbows wide.
  • Think hands toward ears, shoulder blades back and down.
  • Return slowly. Don't let the band snap you forward.

Feel: rear shoulders and upper back.   Not: neck and traps shrugging.

Do thisPull toward your face until your hands finish beside your ears. 12–20 reps.If that’s too difficultStep closer, or use the lightest band.If that’s too easyStep farther back, or pause beside the ears for 2 seconds.
Watch the form

When you repeat this on Day 7

Do not add exercises. Pick one progression per exercise:

  • Use the next heavier band.
  • Shorten the band (step wider, or choke up on the ends).
  • Add a 2-second squeeze at the hard end.
  • Add a 3-second return.
  • Keep tension between reps — never go slack.
  • Add 2–5 reps.
  • Reduce transition time, only if form stays clean.
