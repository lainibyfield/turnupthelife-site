Phase 2 · Lessons 8–21 · coming

Learn to Train and Sustain

How do I increase the training stimulus and keep progressing when life isn't perfect?

The movements are familiar. Now you'll learn different ways to make them more effective — tempo, lengthened holds, mechanical drop sets, cluster sets, density, volume, and other progression methods. Each lesson is a complete workout through which the method is experienced, not just explained. Later lessons apply these tools to busy weeks, travel, small spaces, parks, and limited equipment. The goal is to create a productive stimulus — and learn how to maintain it when conditions change.

Phase 3 · coming

Progress the Load

How do I increase resistance when my available weights are no longer enough?

Now bands become a deliberate loading tool. You'll add bands to dumbbells and kettlebells, progress through different band strengths, and learn appropriate double-banded setups. You'll also learn how to organize these progressions across training weeks: when to add repetitions, when to add band resistance, when to combine methods, and when to reduce the workload. The goal is no longer simply to make one workout harder — it's to make resistance progress in a way that can be tracked and repeated.

Phase 4 · coming

BFR for Hypertrophy

How can lighter loads create a powerful muscle-building stimulus?

Blood flow restriction training introduces a specialized approach to hypertrophy using lighter external loads. You'll learn its purpose, safe application, exercise selection, progression, fatigue management, and how it fits alongside conventional resistance training. This phase comes last on purpose: before using BFR, you'll already understand movement, effort, progression, loading, and recovery. BFR becomes another precise training tool — not a shortcut or a replacement for the foundation you've built.
