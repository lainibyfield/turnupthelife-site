LESSON 1

Welcome

Pilates-informed strength with two 10 lb dumbbells — so you can wine low, jump the road, and wave your flag till the last truck passes. Work through the chapters in order; tick each one done and your progress saves.

Fellas — yes, you too. Same workout, same rules. If 10s truly feel light, start with 20s or 25s and follow every pause and slow count. Then come talk to us.

Brand new to exercise? Start with 10 lb or under — even 5s, even a full water bottle in each hand. Holding on is grip work, and grip counts. Master the movement first; the pounds can wait.

The two labels you'll see

  • Pace — "5 down · 2 hold": seconds to lower, then seconds to hold at the hardest point. Slow is how a 10 turns into a 20.
  • Effort — "1–2 left": stop with one or two good reps left in the tank — not when it gets annoying. "Empty the tank" means the next clean rep won't happen.
  • All you need for this lesson: two dumbbells (≈10 lb) and a sturdy chair. That's the whole list.
  • The five house cues: Ribs down (like you’re keeping someone from stealing your heart — laugh and you can feel it) · Heavy shoulders (your neck isn’t invited) · Grow taller (length first, then strength) · Own the movement, don’t survive it · If you lose control, you’ve already gone too heavy.
  • Optional extras, never required: 15 lb kettlebell (swap: one dumbbell) · box or bench (swap: your chair, a bottom stair, or a very sturdy toilet lid) · ab wheel (swap: slow plank walkouts) · resistance band (swap: slow shoulder circles). The equipment isn't the workout — the control is.
GET LOW

Move Prep

5–7 min

This is where your wine lives — low, loose, and in control, without your knees or back paying for it later.

Before you begin

Today's warm-up prepares your body for today's workout — not for a flexibility contest.

  • Work only in a pain-free range.
  • Hold a chair, wall, or countertop anytime balance limits the movement.
  • Smaller movements done well beat bigger movements done poorly.
  • If something feels sharp, unstable, or wrong, reduce the range or skip that exercise and move on.

Squat & Pistol Prepeasy and controlled — warm, not tired

  • Wide deep-squat hold — 20–30 seconds. watch Mod: hold a chair or countertop, or don't squat as low.
  • Weight shifts in the wide squat — rock side to side, 8 each side. watch
  • Cossack shift (a.k.a. spider-man lunge) — stay low, let your hips travel side to side, 6–8 each way. Keep the working heel down; don't force depth. watchMod: stand a little taller, or hold a chair for balance.
  • Narrow deep-squat hold — 20 seconds. watch Mod: hold support, or place a rolled towel under your heels.
  • Staggered deep-squat hold — 15–20 seconds each side. watch Mod: shorten the stance and reduce the depth.
  • Knee-over-toe touches — 8–10 each side, 2-second pause. watchMod: let the knee travel only as far as comfortable, or hold a chair.
  • Assisted one-leg squat practice (hold something) — 3–5 clean each side. watchMod: use both hands for support, or squat to a higher chair.
  • Shoulder-blade push-ups — 10 reps. watchMod: perform against a wall, countertop, or bench.
  • Band pull-aparts or big shoulder circles — 15 reps. watchMod: use a lighter band, or reduce the range of motion.

Ready to build? If you're breathing a little harder and your joints feel smoother — not tired — you warmed up just right.

Opening the ankles, hips, and inner thighs lets you get low and wine back up without asking one cranky joint to do all the work.

BUILD

Legs

12 min

A1Bulgarian Split Squatquad focus — back foot up on a box, bench, or chair

Pace 5 down · 2 hold Effort empty the tank Reps 8–12 / leg Weight KB or 1 DB

Imagine your zipper stays vertical.

  • Back foot on the box, bench, chair, or step; weight held at your chest.
  • Stay more upright than you think; let the front knee travel forward over the toes.
  • Drive through the middle of the foot — think about straightening the front knee, not pushing your hips back.
  • Let the back leg help only enough to keep your balance.
  • Five seconds down, pause 2 near the bottom, stand up with control.
  • Then set the weight down and keep going bodyweight until your form starts to slip.
Pilates note
Today we’re making this a quad exercise. The RDLs that follow shift the emphasis to the back of the legs.

A2Deficit Single-Leg RDLstanding on the edge of a box for extra reach

Pace 3 down · 3 hold Effort 1–2 left Reps 10–15 / side Weight 2 × 10 DB 🌱 booty fertilizer

Close the car door with your butt.

  • Stand near the edge of the box so the dumbbells can travel below your foot.
  • Hinge from the hips — soft bend in the standing knee, long spine.
  • Reach the hips back.
  • Pause in the stretch 2–3 seconds — stop before your back rounds.
  • Want more? Hold both dumbbells in the hand opposite the working leg.
Easy does it
Don't chase depth if your toe, knee, or hip starts changing the movement. Match reps and range to the stiffer side.
Why this matters

This loads the hamstrings and glutes where they’re longest — one of the best places to build strength and shape.

Personal observation
Your anatomy is your own, but for me, training my inner thighs made a visible difference in the shape of my legs. This is Pilates-informed hypertrophy training — not basic or traditional strength training.

Push

10 min

Push-ups, Three Waysgo until you can't, then make them easier — twice

Pace 4 down · 1 hover · pop up Effort empty the tank Rounds 2–3 Rest 90 sec

Screw your hands into the floor. Pull yourself to the floor. Push the floor away.

  • Four seconds down, hover 1 second off the floor, then pop up.
  • Full push-ups until you can't keep them clean.
  • Straight into push-ups from your knees.
  • Finish holding halfway down as long as you can.
Why this matters

Chest and shoulders are obvious. The surprise is how much your core has to work when you refuse to let your ribs sag.

Pull

10 min

C1One-arm Row Drop Setfull reps, then partials, then a hold — same weight throughout

Pace 5 · 2 · 2 · 2 Effort to failure Weight 15 KB or 1 × 10 DB

Squeeze your shoulder blades together like you’re squeezing the juice out of a rolled mango. (Or a lemon, if you’ve never rolled a mango.)

  • Full reps — 10–12 to technical failure. Pace 5-2-2-2: 5 seconds lowering, 2-second stretch at the bottom, 2 seconds pulling, 2-second squeeze at the top.
  • No kettlebell? Use one 10 lb dumbbell with the same tempo. If you still aren't close to failure, grow the squeeze at the top to 3 seconds before you add reps.
  • Straight into bottom-half partials — 10 slow reps. Start with your arm almost straight, where the lat is at its longest. Pull only until your elbow is about halfway back, then lower again — stay in that stretched bottom half. watch
  • Straight into the top hold. Elbow tight to your side, shoulder blade squeezed toward your back pocket. Hold 20–30 seconds.
  • Switch sides and repeat.
Why this matters

A strong upper back helps you stand taller and keeps your shoulders from doing work your back should be doing.

C2Dumbbell Pulloverlying back across a box or bench

Pace 3 down · 3 stretch Effort 2 left, stay smooth Reps 12–20

Reach until you feel your ribs want to pop — stop just before they do.

  • Lie back across the box or bench, both dumbbells held together.
  • Only as far back as your shoulders are happy with; 3-second stretch at the bottom.
  • Ribs down — don't let this become a lower-back arch.
Why this matters

Think of this as the stretch after the squeeze. Your lats get to work through a longer range instead of staying shortened.

Shoulders

5–7 min

One-Arm Lateral Raise Ladderone arm at a time — little dumbbells, big burn

Rounds 2–3 Effort 1 left Rest 60–75 sec

Lead with your elbows. Heavy shoulders.

  • 10 half raises.
  • As many full raises as you can with good form.
  • Hold 10 seconds at the highest position you can keep with good form.
  • Repeat on the other arm.
Why this matters

Strong shoulders create width; a strong upper back creates posture. Together they build what most people call a “V-taper” — or, more simply, a strong-looking back.

THE 10 LB CHALLENGE

Still think 10s are too light?

Use one pair of dumbbells for the whole workout. Don't shorten the range. Don't skip the pauses. Don't rush.

Gentlemen: your version is 20s or 25s — every pause, every slow count, all the way through. Same rules. No shortcuts.
