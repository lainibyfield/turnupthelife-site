Where the name comes from

Ital-inspired,
never Ital.

The Livitarian Way takes its name from livity — the Rastafari word for a life lived close to the earth. The coinage is deliberate: livitarian names a way of eating the way vegetarian or pescatarian do, built on the root rather than claiming the word itself.

This book borrows livity's wisdom with gratitude — the earth-closeness of the tiers, the small-fish rule, the low-salt kitchen — and says so plainly: it is not a religious text, it does not practice or speak for Rastafari, and where it departs — soy, certified tins, the Practical Exceptions annex — it departs openly.

Naming the debt is the respect. Readers who want the tradition itself should seek out Ital cooks and Rastafari voices directly.

The Tier System

It describes food, not morality. Nothing here is forbidden; the tiers just say where a food sits.

Tier 1

From the earth

Grows with minimal processing: provisions, legumes, greens, fruit, coconut, nuts, seeds, herbs.

Tier 2

Traditional transformations

Keeps the whole food recognizable: tofu, tempeh, soy milk, soy curls.

Tier 3

Small fish, intentional

Carefully sourced: small fish under about a foot — no predators, shellfish, or bivalves — 3× a week or less.

Tier 4

Practical exceptions

Kept in an annex as practical choices, not foundation: rescue food, known-farmer eggs/chicken, US-context TVP. 🏝 Veggie chunks are an island staple and move up in practice.

Label Literacy

Four labels decide most of the nutrition. Read the panel, not the brand.

  • RiceThe bag must say whole grain brown rice, one ingredient. Parboiled/"converted" keeps some nutrients but loses the bran and most fiber. "Rice" defaults to white/parboiled — ask for whole-grain by full name.
  • TofuA true anchor tofu shows 16–20 g protein per 4 oz (super-firm). Extra-firm is close; soft/silken are for sauces and mousse. Buy the firmest available, press hard, pair with a second protein.
  • Plant milkThe plan assumes ≥8 g protein and ~440–450 mg calcium per cup, whatever the brand. Fortified soy milk qualifies fully; unfortified covers protein only.
  • Canned fishA current third-party sustainability certification on the tin plus the origin: sardines from Morocco, Japan, or a certified fishery; skip Mediterranean/Iberian tins regardless of prestige. Rinse all brine.

How to Build a Day

Every recipe declares a protein weight class. A day is one light + one middle + one heavy anchor (or a D+ and lighter elsewhere), plus a module if you're short — drawn from at least three different protein families.

B · 30–35 g · lightest L · 35–40 g · middle D · 40–45 g · heaviest D+ · ~50 g · fused M · 20–25 g · module

No recipe — and no plate size — belongs to a time of day. Pumpkin soup at 7 a.m. is a perfectly built morning. The Planner only cares about weight class and rotation; you decide when to eat what.
