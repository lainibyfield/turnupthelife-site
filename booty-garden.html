What This Is

Not a full program — a booster. Short sessions built around two resistance bands, meant to tack onto a main Movement training day when you've got extra time, or to stand alone on a day the Academy doesn't call for one.

Every lesson starts the same way this one does: you calibrate your equipment before you train with it. A band that's right for someone else's video isn't automatically right for you — five minutes of honest testing beats guessing.

The foundation

Meet the Garden

Your glutes are a team of three, and this course trains all three jobs on purpose:

  1. Create forceDriving through the hips — the engine.
  2. Move the legOut to the side, under control — the steering.
  3. Keep the pelvis steadyWhile the rest of you moves — the frame.

No exercise in this course belongs to only one job. We're changing the task and noticing where you feel the greatest demand — that's the whole method.

Why bands

Grows Anywhere

Heavier glute work usually means a gym: a cable stack for kickbacks, a bench for hip thrusts, a rack for loaded squats. That's real training, but it isn't always reachable — no gym access, no time to drive there, no one to spot you.

Two bands and a stretch of floor cover the same three jobs. A long-loop band worn over the shoulders loads a squat the way a barbell would. A mini band above the knees turns a simple step or kick into real, felt resistance. Nothing to anchor, nothing to book.
