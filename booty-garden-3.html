Feel It Before We Name It

Notice what you feel when you press the band outward, when you control the leg coming back in, when one leg moves, and when one leg stays underneath you.

Today's Movements

You'll use a mini-band lateral walk and a supported standing abduction. Both movements take your leg out to the side. The difference is what stays still.

During the lateral walk, your entire body travels. During standing abduction, your body stays tall while one leg moves.

Learn it

Find the Outer Garden

Standing Abductionband above the knees

Stand tall and hold a wall or another stable surface. Keep your toes facing forward.

Reps 5 slow each side

Move one leg directly out to the side. Do not:

  • lean away
  • turn your toes upward
  • hike your hip
  • swing the leg

Use a range you can control. The goal isn't to lift your foot as high as possible — it's to let the outer hip move the leg.

🌱 Booty Garden

Grow Outward

Set a timer for five minutes. Move continuously through the sequence below. Keep the movement deliberate — the mini band should resist you without deciding how far you're allowed to move.

1. Mini-Band Lateral Walk8 steps right, 8 steps left

Watch · Lateral band walk demonstration

Soften your knees. Send your hips slightly behind you. Keep space between your feet. Control the trailing leg instead of letting the band snap it inward.

Your feet are riding on two separate train tracks. Step sideways without letting the tracks crash into each other.

If this is too difficult, try
  • A lighter mini band.
  • Placing the band above your knees.
  • Taking smaller steps while maintaining space between your feet.
If this is too easy
  • Keep the same band and setup. Complete today's steps with full control. At the end of the lesson, you'll learn when to add a pause.

2. Supported Standing Abduction8 reps right, 8 reps left

Watch · Supported standing abduction

Stand tall. Keep your toes facing forward. Move the leg out and return it slowly.

Move your knee toward the wall beside you, then bring it home quietly. No leaning tower.

If this is too difficult, try
  • A lighter mini band.
  • Keeping the band above your knees.
  • A smaller range while keeping your torso tall.
If this is too easy
  • Keep the same band and setup. Complete today's repetitions with full control. At the end of the lesson, you'll learn when to add a pause.

Continue for Five Minutes

Return to the lateral walk and repeat the sequence until the timer ends.

Wrap-up

How Did Your Garden Grow?

Place your hand on the upper, outer part of the hip that worked. Did you feel it on the leg that moved, the leg that stayed underneath you, or both? Tap to find out what you just trained.Gluteus Medius — abduction

The broad muscle beneath your hand is your gluteus medius. It helps move your leg away from your body, keep your thigh aligned, and control your pelvis while you stand on one leg. Moving the leg out to the side is called abduction — you don't need to memorize the word, just the feeling of creating and controlling space between your legs.

Your moving leg used the outer glutes to press against the band. Your standing leg used its outer glutes to help keep your pelvis over your foot — the garden was working on both sides, even when only one leg appeared to be moving. You may feel this lesson high on the outer hip, and you may notice one side tires sooner or feels less coordinated. That's information, not failure.

Today's job: create and control movement out to the side.

Today's Way to Grow: Add a Pause

Next time, hold the widest part of each step or leg lift for two seconds. Add the pause when you can complete today's repetitions without the band snapping your legs back together.

If you have to lean, twist, or hold your breath to stay there, keep the shorter pause. Today, the repetitions stay the same — only the pause grows.
