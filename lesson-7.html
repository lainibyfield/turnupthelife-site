Lesson 07 · Turn Up the Life

Own
Your Body.

Today's challenge isn't remembering exercises. It's making the spaces between the exercises disappear.

Every movement in this lesson is one you already know. Nothing new to learn, nothing to look up. What's left is flow — changing position without resetting, without hunting, without looking awkward.

About 45–60 min · Two dumbbells · one kettlebell · a band (mini, or a long one tied into a loop) · chair or bench.

First time through
Allow up to 70 minutes while you learn the movements. It gets faster once the order is familiar — the workout doesn't change, your hunting-for-what's-next time disappears. Don't rush the first one to hit a clock.

The last principle — and the one hiding inside it
Pilates names six principles: Breath, Concentration, Centering, Control, Precision, and Flow. You've been practising five of them all along — every time you were told ribs down, move small, stop before the back rounds. Flow is last on purpose: you can't smooth out the transitions while you're still working out what comes next.

And Breath was never a seventh thing to learn. It lives in the pauses. Where you used to stop and reset, you now breathe. That's the whole difference between a workout and a practice.

Move Prep

6–8 min

The same warm-up as every lesson. You know it by heart now — that's the point.

Before you begin

Today's warm-up prepares your body for today's workout — not for a flexibility contest.

  • Work only in a pain-free range.
  • Hold a chair, wall, or countertop anytime balance limits the movement.
  • Smaller movements done well beat bigger movements done poorly.
  • If something feels sharp, unstable, or wrong, reduce the range or skip that exercise and move on.
Timer

Squat & Pistol Prepeasy and controlled — warm, not tired

  • Wide deep-squat hold — 20–30 seconds. watch Mod: hold a chair or countertop, or don't squat as low.
  • Weight shifts in the wide squat — rock side to side, 8 each side. watch
  • Cossack shift (a.k.a. spider-man lunge) — stay low, let your hips travel side to side, 6–8 each way. Keep the working heel down; don't force depth. watchMod: stand a little taller, or hold a chair for balance.
  • Narrow deep-squat hold — 20 seconds. watch Mod: hold support, or place a rolled towel under your heels.
  • Staggered deep-squat hold — 15–20 seconds each side. watch Mod: shorten the stance and reduce the depth.
  • Knee-over-toe touches — 8–10 each side, 2-second pause. watchMod: let the knee travel only as far as comfortable, or hold a chair.
  • Assisted one-leg squat practice (hold something) — 3–5 clean each side. watchMod: use both hands for support, or squat to a higher chair.
  • Shoulder-blade push-ups — 10 reps. watchMod: perform against a wall, countertop, or bench.
  • Band pull-aparts or big shoulder circles — 15 reps. watchMod: use a lighter band, or reduce the range of motion.

Ready to build? If you're breathing a little harder and your joints feel smoother — not tired — you warmed up just right.

Then, transition add-on

No weights. You're not warming muscles here — you're practising changing position without resetting. Move through this twice, slowly:

  • Deep squat → stand
  • Hip hinge → stand
  • Lunge → stand

Watch for the little pause where you gather yourself before standing. That pause is what today is about — first you notice it, then you breathe in it, then it disappears.

Core

5–7 min

Same three as last time. Nothing to learn — just do them better than you did before.

Timer

Bear Crawlleft and right, learning to take turns

Move quietly.

Sets 3Time 20–30 secWeight bodyweight
  • Hands under shoulders, knees under hips and hovering an inch off the floor.
  • Opposite hand and opposite foot move together — that pairing is the whole exercise.
  • Hips stay level. If they rock side to side, make the steps smaller.
  • Quiet hands and quiet feet mean you're in control.

Feel: deep trunk working to stop the rocking.   Not: shoulders burning out or hips swinging.

Do this3 rounds of 20–30 seconds of crawling, opposite hand and foot together.If that’s too difficultStay in the hover — knees an inch off the floor, no travel yet.If that’s too easyCrawl forward and backward without stopping between directions.
Watch the form

Offset Dead Bugone dumbbell, one hand — the first anti-rotation work

Don't let the weight twist you.

Sets 2Reps 8–10 / sideWeight 1 DB, one hand

SetupOn your back, knees stacked over hips. Hold one dumbbell straight up over your shoulder in one hand. The other arm rests on the floor. The load is uneven on purpose.

  • Set the shoulders first, ribs down, low back gently connected to the floor.
  • Extend the leg opposite the hand holding the dumbbell.
  • The weight on one side is trying to tip and turn you. Refuse — both shoulder blades stay down.
  • Complete all the repetitions, then switch sides and repeat.

Feel: the side of your trunk working to stay square.   Not: ribs popping or the low back arching off the floor.

Do thisOne dumbbell held up in one hand, extend the opposite leg. 8–10 per side, ribs down throughout.If that’s too difficultPut the dumbbell down. Same offset pattern, bodyweight only — one arm reaching overhead, opposite leg long.If that’s too easySlide the leg further out, or hold the extended position for 2 seconds before returning.
Watch the form

Side Planklateral core — hip and rib stack

Lift the underside of your waist.

Sets 2Hold 20–40 sec / side
  • Stack the ribs over the hips; no sagging into the bottom shoulder.

Coming down
To come down, place your top hand on the floor before lowering your hips.

Do thisSide plank from the knees, 20–40 seconds per side.If that’s too difficultBend your knees further underneath you, or shorten the hold to 15 seconds.If that’s too easyLift the top leg, or extend the hold to 45 seconds.

Step 3 / 7THE LESSON

Movement Flow

10–12 min

This block isn't for fatigue. It's for smoothness. If you finish it out of breath, you went too fast.

Timer

Suitcase Carryone weight, one side, walking tall 🌱 fertilizer

Walk like you're carrying groceries without spilling your coffee.

Sets 2Distance 30–40 steps / sideWeight 1 KB or DB
  • One weight, hanging at one side. Shoulders level, ribs down.
  • Walk slowly and deliberately — this is not a race to the other wall.
  • Finish all the steps on one side, then switch hands and walk back.

Feel: one side of your trunk working harder than the other.   Not: leaning toward or away from the weight.

Do thisCarry one dumbbell or kettlebell for 30–40 slow, controlled steps. Walk tall and let your body resist the pull of the weight.If that’s too difficultUse a lighter weight, shorten the distance, or lightly touch a wall for the first few steps until you find your balance.If that’s too easyCarry the weight overhead, or pause for one second every third step without letting your trunk lean.
Watch the form

Transition Practicethe flow itself — no timer, no sets

Can you change positions without looking awkward?

3 rounds. Learn the sequence — don't chase fatigue.
  1. Suitcase carry — a short walk
  2. Park the weight (breathe)
  3. Contralateral single-leg RDL
  4. Stand tall (breathe)
  5. Down to half-kneeling
  6. One press
  7. Stand tall (breathe)
  8. Walk it out
  9. Repeat on the opposite side
The greyed steps aren't rests. They're the transitions — the part you're actually practising. Breathe there instead of resetting.

Very little fatigue on purpose
Light weights, few rounds. If you're gasping, you turned a flow practice into a conditioning circuit. Slow it down.

Do thisMove through the entire sequence without rushing. Pause only long enough to change positions safely.If that’s too difficultBreak the flow into two smaller sequences and practise each separately before linking them together.If that’s too easyComplete the flow continuously for 3–5 rounds, breathing steadily and never stopping between positions.

Step 4 / 7BUILD

Legs

14–16 min

Familiar work, done well. One thing is new at the end — and it's the one you've been earning.

Timer

A1Contralateral Single-Leg RDLone weight — in the opposite hand 🌱 fertilizer

Close the car door with your butt.

Sets 2Reps 8–10 / sideWeight 1 KB, opposite handPace 3 down · 2 stretch · controlled rise

One weight again — but the other hand
You've done this hinge with the weight on the same side you were standing on, and heavier than this with two dumbbells. Today the kettlebell is in the hand opposite your standing leg. You'll expect it to be easier. Try one rep before you decide.

  • Stand on your right leg, kettlebell in your left hand. Then swap.
  • Hinge from the hips, soft bend in the standing knee, long spine.
  • The weight will try to pull that shoulder down and turn you. Stay square to the floor.
  • Feel the line from the working hip to the opposite shoulder — that diagonal is doing the job.

Feel: hamstring and glute, plus the whole diagonal across your back.   Not: twisting open toward the weight.

Do thisOne kettlebell in the hand opposite your standing leg. 8–10 per side, staying square.If that’s too difficultLet the working-leg toe touch down slightly behind your hip plane for balance — same hinge, same weight, just a kickstand.If that’s too easyStand on a jump box or step and let the weight reach past your foot for a deeper stretch.
Watch the form

A2Front-Foot Elevated Split Squatnow the working sets 🌱 fertilizer

Imagine your zipper stays vertical.

Sets 3Reps 8–12 / legWeight 1 DB beside the front legPace 3 down · 1 pause · controlled riseEffort 1–2 good reps left
  • Front foot elevated, one dumbbell hanging beside the front leg.
  • Resist the pull toward the weighted side the whole set.
  • Drive through the middle of the front foot.
Do thisFront foot elevated, one dumbbell beside the front leg. 8–12 per leg.If that’s too difficultHold a chair or wall with your free hand, or put the dumbbell down and go bodyweight.If that’s too easySlow the lowering to 5 seconds, or elevate the front foot a little higher.
Watch the form

A3Hip Thrustnow you can actually load the glute 🌱 fertilizer

Ribs down, chin down, push the floor away through your heels.

Sets 3Reps 10–15Weight 1 DB or KB across the hipsPace 2 up · 2 squeeze · 3 downEffort 1–2 good reps left

SetupUpper back resting on a bench, sofa edge, or box. Feet flat, shins vertical at the top. Weight across the hips — pad it with a folded towel.

  • Tuck the ribs down before you lift; don't arch the back to get higher.
  • Chin stays tucked, eyes forward — your gaze travels with your hips.
  • Squeeze at the top for 2, shins vertical, then lower with control.

Feel: glutes doing all of it.   Not: low back or hamstrings taking over.

Why this replaces the frog pumps
Frog pumps did their job — they taught you to find the squeeze with almost no load. You can find it now without thinking. So this is where you finally load it properly.

Do thisUpper back on a bench, weight across the hips. 10–15 reps with a 2-second squeeze at the top.If that’s too difficultTake the weight off and do a floor glute bridge — same squeeze, no bench.If that’s too easySingle-leg, bodyweight or light — one foot planted, the other knee drawn in.

Step 5 / 7BUILD

Push

8–10 min

Unchanged from last time. Same two presses — just smoother getting into and out of them.

Timer

B1Half-Kneeling One-Arm Presship connects to the opposite shoulder

Press from your back pocket, not your neck.

Sets 2Reps 6–10 / armWeight 1 DB
  • Half-kneeling: one knee down, other foot forward, both at right angles.
  • Press with the arm on the same side as the down knee — that's what makes the trunk connect hip to shoulder.
  • Squeeze the glute of the down leg before you press. Ribs stay down.
  • Don't lean away from the weight to get it up.

Feel: the side of your trunk and the down-side glute holding you upright.   Not: low back arching or the ribs flaring.

Do thisHalf-kneeling, one dumbbell, pressing on the same side as the down knee. 6–10 per arm.If that’s too difficultPress from both knees, or lighten the dumbbell until the ribs stay down.If that’s too easySlow the lowering to 4 seconds, or lift the down-side knee an inch off the floor for the last few reps.
Watch the form

B2Single-Arm Floor Pressone side works, the whole trunk holds

Press without rolling.

Sets 3Reps 8–12 / armWeight 1 DBPace 3 down · 1 touch · press up strongEffort 1–2 good reps left
  • On your back, knees bent, feet flat, one dumbbell in one hand.
  • Lower until the upper arm lightly touches the floor, then press.
  • The weight on one side wants to roll you toward it. Keep both shoulder blades on the floor.
  • Free hand rests on your belly so you can feel the ribs stay down.

Rib caution
Keep the ribs closed the whole set — like the moment right after a laugh or a cough. Don't let them pop open as the weight gets heavy.

Do thisPress one arm while the other arm rests on the floor. 8–12 per arm.If that’s too difficultPress with both dumbbells at the same time instead of one arm — same weight, less rotational demand.If that’s too easyKeep one arm extended while the other presses. Alternate sides.

Step 6 / 7BUILD

Pull

6–8 min

The pulling you know, then one heavier walk to finish.

Timer

C1Split-Stance One-Arm Rowopposite foot forward — the diagonal again

Pull into your back pocket.

Sets 3Reps 10–12 / armWeight 1 DBPace 3 down · pause · controlled pull
  • Split stance: the foot opposite your rowing arm steps forward.
  • Hinge at the hips, free hand braced on a chair or your thigh.
  • Pull the elbow back toward your back pocket; keep the torso still.
  • No twisting to move the weight — the stance is what makes this cross-body.

Feel: mid-back and lat, with the opposite hip helping you stay square.   Not: shoulder shrugging or the torso rotating open.

Do thisOpposite foot forward, one dumbbell, 10–12 per arm with the torso still.If that’s too difficultBrace your free hand on a higher surface so you hinge less, or use a lighter dumbbell.If that’s too easyHold the squeeze for 2 seconds at the top of every rep.
Watch the form

C2Band Pull-Apartsupper-body fertilizer — you already know this one

Straight arms. Let the shoulder blades do the work.

Sets 2Reps 15–20Band lightPace 2 pull · 2 squeeze · 3 return
  • Arms straight and level at chest height.
  • Open the band by driving the shoulder blades together and down.
  • Return slowly. Don't let the band snap you closed.
Do this15–20 reps, 2 pull · 2 squeeze · 3 return.If that’s too difficultWiden your grip on the band.If that’s too easyNarrow your grip on the band.

Farmer Carry Finishone heavier lap — nothing to learn, just posture 🌱 fertilizer

Tall, level, quiet. One lap.

Sets 1Distance one lap · 40–60 stepsWeight 2 DBs or KBs — heavier than the suitcase carry
  • A weight in each hand. Pick them up, walk, put them down.
  • Shoulders back and down, ribs stacked over hips, eyes up.
  • Walk until your grip tells you to stop, then set them down under control.

Not teaching — practising
There's nothing new here. It's one honest lap with something heavy so you finish the course standing tall.

Do thisOne lap, 40–60 steps, a weight in each hand, walking tall.If that’s too difficultLighter weights, or split it into two shorter laps with a break to reset your grip.If that’s too easyHeavier weights, or walk until your grip genuinely gives out rather than counting steps.

Step 7 / 7RISE

Rise

6–8 min

Don't stretch yet. Walk first.

Timer

One Slow Victory Lapone minute · no phone · no timer

Just walk. Notice what your body is doing without being told.

Time about 1 minuteWeight none
  • Your arm swing — is it even on both sides?
  • Opposite arm with opposite foot — the thing you trained, happening on its own.
  • Your breathing — settling without you managing it.
  • Your posture — taller than when you started, and you didn't have to think about it.

Why no timer here
Every other timed thing in this course exists to keep you honest. This one is the opposite — the last minute of the last lesson isn't work. Walk until you feel like stopping.

Then stretchfour pieces, unhurried

  • Horse stance — 30 seconds. Sit down between your feet, chest proud. watch
  • Hip flexor stretch — 30 seconds each side. Squeeze the back glute and stand tall. watch
  • Forward fold — 30 seconds. Soft knees, heavy head.
  • Cross-body reaches — 10 total, slow. Let the ribs turn.

🥤 That's the last lesson. Drink your water — you own this now.

That's the whole courseSeven lessons, one method. You started by learning to own the weight; you finish owning the spaces in between. Now go run the road.
