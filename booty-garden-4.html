Today's Movements

You'll use a supported single-leg clock tap and a mini-band lateral step and hold. These aren't about taking the biggest step — they're about controlling the leg that stays underneath you.

Find the Roots of Your Garden

Supported Single-Leg Clock Tapbodyweight, no band

Stand beside a wall or stable surface. Place most of your weight on your left foot. Soften your left knee and hold that bend the whole round — don't let it straighten between taps. Keep your pelvis level.

Imagine you're balancing a full watering can on your standing hip. Keep that hip level while your other foot reaches around the garden.

Rounds 2 slow each side

Use your right toes to tap:

  • in front of you
  • out to the side
  • behind you

Return to the center after each tap — but don't let the standing knee straighten to rest between taps. That sustained bend is what makes the standing-side glutes do the work.

Pay attention to the standing leg — it's doing more than waiting. It's organizing your hip, thigh, knee, and foot while the other leg moves.

Deep Roots

Place your mini band above your knees. Set a timer for five minutes. Move continuously through the sequence below, slowly enough to notice every correction your body makes.

1. Supported Single-Leg Clock Tapmini band above the knees · 3 rounds each side

Watch · Single-leg clock tap demonstration

Stand primarily on your left leg. Tap the right toes forward, to the side, then behind. Complete 3 rounds, then switch sides and complete 3 rounds. Keep the standing knee aligned with the middle of the foot. Keep your pelvis as level and quiet as possible.

Your standing leg is the tree. The other foot is drawing a clock around its roots.

If this is too difficult, try
  • Removing the mini band — you learned this move bodyweight-only, and that's a completely valid way to do it.
  • Keeping the taps closer to your standing foot.
  • Using more support, placing the tapping foot down between directions.
If this is too easy
  • Keep the same setup. Complete today's taps with control. At the end of the lesson, you'll learn when to reach farther.

2. Lateral Step and Hold6 steps right, 6 steps left

Watch · Lateral step-and-hold demonstration

After every step, hold for two seconds. During the hold: keep space between your knees, keep your pelvis level, keep your weight over the working foot, and don't let the band pull you inward.

Step sideways and freeze for the photo. Don't let the band smush your knees together before the picture is taken.

If this is too difficult, try
  • A lighter mini band.
  • Placing the band above your knees.
  • A smaller step or a shorter hold.
If this is too easy
  • Keep the same band and setup. Complete today's steps with control. At the end of the lesson, you'll learn when to reach farther.

Continue for Five Minutes

Return to the clock taps and repeat the sequence until the timer ends.

Wrap-up

How Did Your Garden Grow?

Place your hand on the side of the hip that worked to keep you steady. You may feel a broad area instead of one obvious muscle. Tap to find out what you just trained.Gluteus Minimus — stability

Deep underneath the muscle you met in the last lesson is your gluteus minimus — the smallest and deepest glute muscle. It works with the gluteus medius to steady your pelvis, control your thigh, and keep you balanced over one leg. You can't completely separate the two by feel; the name belongs to the deeper part of the team you just asked to stabilize you.

The previous lesson emphasized moving out to the side. This lesson emphasized controlling your pelvis while your weight shifted from one leg to the other. The gluteus medius still helped — so did the gluteus maximus. Your glutes are a team, but today the deepest part of the garden had a larger reason to participate.

Today's job: keep the pelvis and thigh steady while the body moves.

Today's Way to Grow: Increase the Range

Next time, reach the tapping foot or lateral step slightly farther away. Increase the range when your standing knee stays lined up with your foot and your pelvis stays level through today's smaller reach.

If the bigger reach turns into a wobble, knee collapse, or hip hike, return to the smaller one. Today, the band and repetitions stay the same — only the useful range grows.
