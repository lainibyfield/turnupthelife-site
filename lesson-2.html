Lesson 02 · Turn Up the Life

Drive
the Hips.

Drive the Hips teaches you how to make light weight harder by creating power from the strongest part of your body. The hips start the movement. The core organizes it. The arms and legs finish without stealing the job.

This is not a cardio day. This is not flinging weights around. This is controlled power: hinge, snap, float, land, reset.

Lesson 1 taught you to own the weight. Lesson 2 teaches you to move it with power — and still stay in charge.

≈45–55 min · Two 10 lb dumbbells · mini-band · chair or bench · optional kettlebell.

GET LOW

Move Prep

5–7 min

Ground the hips first — closed and familiar. The flow you know, then two new rotational pieces before the hips drive.

Before you begin

Today's warm-up prepares your body for today's workout — not for a flexibility contest.

  • Work only in a pain-free range.
  • Hold a chair, wall, or countertop anytime balance limits the movement.
  • Smaller movements done well beat bigger movements done poorly.
  • If something feels sharp, unstable, or wrong, reduce the range or skip that exercise and move on.

Cossack Shiftalso called spider-man lunge · deep lateral hip mobility

Stay low. Let your hips travel side to side.

  • Shift your weight from one leg to the other without standing up.
  • Keep the heel down on the working leg as much as comfortable.
  • Don't force the depth.

Mod: stand a little taller, or hold a chair for balance.

THE LESSON

Hip Power

8–10 min

Power happens while you're fresh — swing and landing mechanics are sharpest early. Hinge, snap, float, land, reset. Rest fully; every rep crisp.

1Two-Hand Swinghip snap — kettlebell or one dumbbell 🌱 fertilizer

Jump without leaving the floor.

Sets 3Reps 12–15Effort crisp reps onlyWeight KB or 1 DB, both hands
  • Feet hip-width to slightly wider.
  • Hinge back; don't squat it.
  • Snap the hips forward and let the weight float.
  • Arms stay loose. Hips do the work.
  • Stop if your low back starts talking.

Form note
If it feels like a shoulder raise, reset. The swing comes from the hinge.

Field test

Did your arms stay relaxed? They should just be ropes — the hips do everything.

Watch · Mark Wildman

2Controlled Step-Downeccentric knee control — own the landing

Own the landing.

Sets 2–3Reps 8–10 / sidePace 5 down · light tap · stand tallEffort controlled every repWeight bodyweight
  • Stand on a 12-inch box or step.
  • Lower one foot to the floor over 5 seconds, lightly tap, then stand back up.
  • Keep the knee tracking over the middle toes.
  • Hold a wall or railing if needed — better that than losing control.

Feel: front-thigh control on the way down.   Not: knee caving in or dropping fast.

Knee note
Lower the step or shorten the range if the knee feels sharp. This is the brake that protects the skater-hop landings next.

Watch the form

3Skater Hop to Stickside-to-side power with a quiet landing

Land, own it, then go again.

Sets 2–3Reps 6–8 / sideEffort crisp reps onlyImpact low if needed
  • Hop side to side and stick the landing before the next rep.
  • Keep the knee tracking the same direction as the toes.
  • Smaller hop is fine. Toe tap is fine. Holding a wall or chair is fine.
  • Better that than tweaking your old knee trying to impress nobody.
  • Land quietly enough so that nobody downstairs complains.
Field test

Could you own every landing before taking the next jump? Reset if not.

BUILD

Legs

15–18 min

Strength → squeeze → lengthened loading → stabilization. Now that the hips know how to drive, load them slowly.

A1Bulgarian Split Squatquad focus — back foot up on a box, bench, or chair

Imagine your zipper stays vertical.

Sets 1–2 working / legReps 8–12 / legPace 5 down · 2 hold · controlled upEffort hard, but cleanWeight 1 DB or KB at chest
  • Back foot on the chair, box, or step; weight held at your chest.
  • Stay more upright than you think; let the front knee travel forward.
  • Drive through the middle of the foot.
  • Five seconds down, pause 2 near the bottom, stand up with control.
  • Set the weight down and keep going bodyweight until form starts to slip.

Feel: front thigh, not low back.   Not: sharp knee pain or low-back strain.

Knee note
If your knee feels sharp or sketchy, shorten the range or keep the back foot on the floor.

Make it easier: keep the back foot on the floor (split squat).
If too easy: add the bodyweight burnout after the weighted reps.

A2Frog Pumpssmall glute bridge — soles of feet together, knees open 🌱 fertilizer

Small move. Big squeeze.

Pace 2 squeeze · 2 lowerEffort glute burn, not back strainSets 2–3Reps 30Weight bodyweight first · 1 DB only if needed
  • Lie on your back, soles of feet together, knees open.
  • Lift the hips, squeeze the glutes, lower with control.
  • Don't arch your back to make the lift bigger.
  • If you feel this in your low back, make it smaller or switch to a regular glute bridge.
Field test

Does this produce enough glute burn to earn a permanent spot? You decide.

A3Deficit Single-Leg RDLhamstring and glute loading — standing on a box, step, or low platform 🌱 fertilizer

Close the car door with your butt.

Sets 1–2 working / sidePace 3 down · 3 stretchEffort 1–2 good reps leftReps 10–12 / sideWeight 2 DBs
  • Stand near the edge of the box so the dumbbells can travel below your foot.
  • Soft bend in the standing knee; hinge from the hips.
  • Reach the hips back; keep the spine long.
  • Pause in the stretch, then stand up with control.
  • Touch a wall or chair if balance starts stealing the exercise.

Back note
Don't chase the floor. If your back rounds, you went too low.

Field test

Did flexibility stop you before strength did? That tells you what to work on.

Watch the form

A4Hip Airplane Holdsingle-leg hip control — hold on if needed

Grow taller before you rotate.

Hold 2 / side · 20 sec Effort controlled, not heroicSupport chair, wall, or counter allowed
  • Stand on one leg with a soft knee.
  • Hinge slightly forward and open the hip.
  • Keep the standing knee tracking forward.
  • Hold the cleanest position you can control.
  • Use support. This is hip work, not a balance stunt.

Knee note
If the knee caves in, make the movement smaller or hold on.

BUILD

Push

8–10 min

Dumbbell Floor Presschest press from the floor

Touch the floor. Don't crash into it.

Pace 4 down · 1 floor touch · press up strongEffort 1–2 good reps leftSets 2–3Reps 10–15Weight 2 DBs
  • Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
  • Hold one dumbbell in each hand.
  • Lower until the upper arms lightly touch the floor.
  • Pause, then press up strong.
  • Keep ribs down; don't turn this into a back arch.

If too easy
Do the same press in a glute bridge — hips stay lifted the whole set. That's the next card.

Watch the form

Glute Bridge Floor Pressfloor press with hips lifted — the harder option for Lesson 2

Ribs down. Hips high. Don't let your butt negotiate.

Pace 3–4 down · 1 floor touch · press up strongEffort 1–2 good reps leftSets 2–3Reps 8–12Weight 2 DBs
  • Set up like the floor press.
  • Lift the hips and hold the bridge.
  • Press while keeping the hips level.
  • If the low back takes over, lower the hips and return to regular floor press.

Note
Save the deeper-range box press for later.

BUILD

Upper-Back Reset

3–4 min

Shoulder reset after pressing — not the back workout. This keeps the shoulders organized once the pressing is done.

Band Pull-Apartsshoulder reset after pressing

Pull your shoulder blades back into the conversation.

Pace 2 pull · 2 squeeze · 3 returnEffort 3–4 reps leftSets 2Reps 15–20Weight light-to-moderate band
  • Hold the band at chest height.
  • Pull it apart until the shoulder blades move toward each other.
  • Pause, then return slowly.
  • Don't let the band snap you back.
  • Heavy shoulders. Your neck is still not invited.
