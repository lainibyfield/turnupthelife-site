Back to the Booty Garden

Your booty is made of three glute muscles: gluteus maximus, gluteus medius, and gluteus minimus.

They do not work alone. Every time you walk, squat, climb, balance, or change direction, all three parts of your garden cooperate. But changing the movement can change which part does more of the work.

Over the next three lessons, you'll learn how to emphasize:

  • the large muscle that creates most of your booty's size and power
  • the upper and outer muscle that helps shape and control your hips
  • the deeper muscle that helps keep your pelvis steady

No anatomy degree required. You're going to learn by moving.

The Long-Loop Hip Thrust

Set Up the Bandself-anchored — no equipment to attach to

Watch · Bret Contreras band hip thrust

Most popular demonstrations anchor the band to equipment. Watch for the thrust itself, then use the self-anchored setup taught below.

Needs long-loop band Needs a firm couch, bench, or platform Needs open floor space

If your support can move, place it against a wall before you begin.

  • Sit on the floor with your upper back against your support.
  • Place both feet inside the long loop.
  • Position one side of the loop underneath the middle of both feet.
  • Bring the opposite side of the loop across the crease at the front of your hips.
  • Keep the band flat, centered across your hips, and securely trapped beneath your feet.

The band should now travel: under both feet, alongside your legs, and across the front of your hips.

Check Yourself Against These

This setup is new, so check each of these before your first repetition — not just the first time, every time:

  1. Both feet have entered the loop.
  2. The band sits beneath both feet, not off to one side.
  3. The band crosses the front of your hip crease — flat, not twisted.
  4. Pressing your feet down and pulling gently against the band doesn't shift, roll, or slip it.
Before you lift Wear shoes that let the band stay secure. Don't rest it against a sharp edge or set it up so it can roll off your feet. Press both feet firmly into the floor and gently pull against the band before your first rep — if it shifts, rolls, or feels like it could slip, stop and reset it.
To exit safely Lower your hips back to the floor first — this slackens the band. Then slide one foot out of the loop at a time. Never stand up while your feet are still inside a stretched band.

Find Your Position

  • Place the lower edge of your shoulder blades against the support.
  • Set your feet about hip-width apart.
  • At the top of the thrust, your shins should be close to vertical.
  • Keep your chin gently tucked.
  • Bring your ribs toward your pelvis instead of opening your chest and arching your back.

Your hips are an elevator. They travel up and down — not up and back. At the top, imagine your belt buckle tipping slightly toward your chin. You're finishing the thrust, not trying to win a backbend contest.

Practice the Movement

Reps 5 slow
  1. Lower your hips with control.
  2. Press both feet into the floor.
  3. Drive your hips forward into the band.
  4. Finish with your ribs and pelvis stacked.
  5. Pause for one second at the top.

You should feel the band become more demanding as your hips rise. You should feel your glutes finish the movement — not your lower back.

Check Your Long-Loop Band

Keep this band if
  • The setup remains secure.
  • You can use the full intended range.
  • The band becomes meaningfully harder near the top.
  • You could perform only a few more controlled repetitions.
Use a lighter band if
  • You cannot arrange it safely.
  • It pulls your feet out of position.
  • You cannot reach the top of the thrust.
  • You must arch your back to finish.
  • You cannot control the lowering portion.
Try a stronger band if
  • It provides very little resistance at the top.
  • All five repetitions feel easy.
  • You could perform many more repetitions without slowing down.

Find the Back of Your Garden

Mini-Band Standing Kickbackband above the knees

Stand tall and hold a wall or another stable surface. Keep a soft bend in your standing knee.

Reps 5 slow each side

Move one leg behind you without:

  • throwing your chest forward
  • arching your lower back
  • turning your toes outward
  • swinging the leg

The movement may be small. That's fine — your goal is to feel the back of your hip shorten as your leg travels behind you.

1. Long-Loop Hip Thrust8 repetitions

Upper back against a stable bench, couch, or padded platform. One side of the long loop securely beneath both feet, the opposite side across the crease at the front of your hips — flat, balanced, unable to slip before you begin.

  • Lower your hips with control.
  • Press through your feet.
  • Drive your hips into the band.
  • Finish with ribs and pelvis stacked, not an arched lower back.
  • Pause briefly at the top.

Send your hips up like an elevator, then show your belt buckle to your chin.

Wrap-up

How Did Your Garden Grow?

Point to where you felt the thrust. Now point to where you felt the kickback. Was it the same place? Tap to find out what you just trained.Gluteus Maximus — hip extension

The large muscle across the back of your booty is your gluteus maximus — the largest muscle in your garden, creating much of the size you see from the back and side. Its biggest job is hip extension: driving your hips forward, or moving your thigh behind you.

During the thrust, you challenged it most as your hips approached the top. During the kickback, you challenged it by moving your thigh behind you. You may have felt other muscles helping — that's normal. The goal isn't to isolate one muscle completely; it's to recognize the job that received the greatest emphasis.

Today's job: create force through hip extension.

Today's Way to Grow: Add Repetitions

Next time, try 10 repetitions instead of 8. Add those repetitions when you can finish today's 8 with the full movement and know you had at least 3 more good ones available.

If repetition 8 already makes you shorten, swing, or arch, stay at 8. Today, the only thing growing is the number of good repetitions.
