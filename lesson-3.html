Lesson 03 · Travel Workout 1

Create
Tension.

A band only feels heavy if you make it heavy. That's the whole lesson. Tension comes from where you stand, how much slack you take out, how slowly you return, and how long you squeeze — not from the band alone.

Lesson 2 taught you to move weight with power. Lesson 3 teaches you to create resistance out of thin air — so a backpack full of bands can replace a gym.

PrincipleMake bands feel heavy.
RoleFull body, biased toward pull and upper back.
TimeAbout 45–60 minutes.
EquipmentLight, medium, and heavy long bands or tubes. No anchor required.
MoodNew · travel · self-directed.

First time through
Allow up to 70 minutes while you learn the movements. It gets faster once the order is familiar — the workout doesn't change, your hunting-for-what's-next time disappears. Don't rush the first one to hit a clock.

Need the band rules? Tap here

Which band for what:

  • Light band — shoulders, pull-aparts, lateral raises.
  • Medium band — chest press, overhead press, face pulls.
  • Heavy band — squats, rows, good mornings, glute bridges.
  • A set only counts if the last few reps are hard and clean.
  • 25 easy reps = the band is too light or too slack. Step wider, shorten the band, take out slack.

Equipment note. This workout assumes long bands or tubes. Packed only mini-loops? The squats, rows, presses, and good mornings need a swap — the band setups won't work as written.

How travel week works

Two workouts, each done twice. Not four new workouts.

Day 1Create Tension
Day 2Rest — walk, swim, shop, enjoy the trip
Day 3Pack Light, Train Hard
Day 4Rest — fun movement
Day 5Create Tension — progressed
Day 6Rest — fun movement
Day 7Pack Light, Train Hard — progressed

"Progressed" never means new exercises. Pick one per exercise: next heavier band · shorten the band · add a 2-second squeeze · add a 3-second return · keep tension between reps · add 2–5 reps · shorten transitions only if form stays clean.

Both workouts once ≈ 10 hard sets per major muscle group. Both twice ≈ 20. That's enough. Don't turn your trip into punishment.

Band Rulesread first

Bands don't work like dumbbells. A dumbbell weighs the same everywhere; a band gets harder the further you stretch it. These six rules make every exercise in this lesson work properly.

  1. Start with moderate tension — not slack, not stretched to the limit.
  2. The middle anchor point is the default unless an exercise says otherwise — and in this lesson your anchor is always your own body: under the middle of your feet, or across your back.
  3. Stand close enough to finish each movement with control. If the last rep snaps you around, you're too far away.
  4. Cross the bands if they feel more stable — especially on lateral raises.
  5. If the band rubs your neck or shoulders, widen your grip or shorten your range.
  6. Choose the easiest variation that lets you feel the intended muscle — not the hardest one you can survive.

Use the band you actually own
No mini-band? Tie a long band into a loop — that's all a mini-band is. Only got a thick, heavy band? Take a shorter grip, or step closer to whatever it's anchored to, until the starting tension feels moderate. The band you have is the right band; adjust the length, not the exercise.

Setting band length
Choose the band position that gives you moderate tension at the starting position — not slack, and not already stretched to its limit. Everything else follows from this.

Why cross the bands (the X)
Standing on a band with one end in each hand, each side pulls straight up its own line. The two lines are parallel and independent — so if one arm is stronger or drifts slightly, the band slides under your foot and the tension shifts to one side. Crossing the bands into an X means each hand pulls against the opposite foot. The lines cross at your middle and brace against each other, so the pull is anchored to your centre instead of to each foot separately. The band stops wandering and both arms get the same tension. X-band is the default; single-arm is the modification.

Move Prep

5 min

This is where your wine lives — low, loose, and in control, without your knees or back paying for it later. Same warm-up every lesson: learn it once, own it forever.

Before you begin

Today's warm-up prepares your body for today's workout — not for a flexibility contest.

  • Work only in a pain-free range.
  • Hold a chair, wall, or countertop anytime balance limits the movement.
  • Smaller movements done well beat bigger movements done poorly.
  • If something feels sharp, unstable, or wrong, reduce the range or skip that exercise and move on.

  • Knee-over-toe touches — 8–10 each side, 2-second pause. watchMod: let the knee travel only as far as comfortable, or hold a chair.
  • Assisted one-leg squat practice (hold something) — 3–5 clean each side. watchMod: use both hands for support, or squat to a higher chair.

Ready to build? If you're breathing a little harder and your joints feel smoother — not tired — you warmed up just right.

Then, band-day add-on

Grab your bands and do a few light warm-up reps of today's first move — a couple of easy banded squats or rows — just to feel the tension before you load it. New tool, same body.

Core

5–6 min

Protect the back. Core is spinal organization, not ab punishment.

Timer

Band-Resisted Dead Bugdeep core — ribs and pelvis control

Same statue. Now the band is pulling on it.

Sets 2Reps 8–10 / sideBand light or medium

SetupWrap the band across your upper back and hold one end in each hand. Press your hands gently apart to create steady tension, and keep that tension the whole way through the dead bug. No anchor, no door, nothing to attach.

  • Set up exactly as you learned it in Lesson 1 — shoulder blades down and together, belly button to spine, gap closed, torso frozen.
  • Hold the band shoulder-width and gently pull it apart throughout the movement. That steady outward press is the resistance.
  • The band adds a pull that's trying to break that. Don't let it.
  • Exhale as the leg reaches. Stop if the low back starts taking over.

How far to reach
Reach only as far overhead as you can while keeping your ribs down and your shoulders comfortable. More range is not better. The goal is a quiet torso — not a full overhead reach.

Feel: deep belly, ribs pinned.   Not: low back arching off the floor.

Do thisKnees stacked over hips, band across your upper back, hands pressing gently apart for steady tension. Reach one leg at a time, 8–10 per side.If that's too difficultKeep your arms pointed at the ceiling and slide your heel along the floor instead of reaching the leg long.If that's too easyAdd the opposite arm reaching overhead as the leg extends — only as far as the ribs stay down.

SetupBand wraps around your upper back, one end in each hand. No anchor needed — your own back is the anchor.

Pull

10–12 min

Today's bias. This is where bands genuinely beat dumbbells — the tension keeps climbing exactly where your back is strongest.

Timer

C1Band Rowmain pull work

Don't let the band win on the way back.

Sets 4Reps 12–20Band medium or heavyPace 2 pull · 2 squeeze · 3 return

SetupSit on the floor with the band around both feet, one end in each hand. Or stand hinged forward with the band under both feet.

  • Sit with the band around your feet, or stand hinged with it under your feet.
  • Pull the elbows back.
  • Squeeze the shoulder blades — like squeezing the juice out of a rolled mango.
  • Return slowly and keep tension the whole way.

Feel: mid-back between the shoulder blades.   Not: biceps doing all the work.

Do thisUnder the middle of your foot is the default. Row 10–20 reps.If that's too difficultSit on the floor, band around both feet, and row from seated.If that's too easyStagger your stance and step back for more tension, or hold the squeeze 2 seconds.
Watch the form

C2Band Pull-Apartupper-back and rear-shoulder reset — nothing to attach

Straight arms. Let the shoulder blades do the work.

Sets 2Reps 15–25Band lightPace 2 open · 2 squeeze · 3 returnEffort 2–3 good reps left

SetupHold the band in both hands at chest height, arms straight out in front of you. Your grip width is your resistance setting — that is the whole setup. Nothing to anchor, nothing to attach.

  • Arms straight and level at chest height.
  • Open the band by driving the shoulder blades together and down.
  • Keep the ribs down — don't lean back to open further.
  • Return slowly. Don't let the band snap you closed.

Feel: rear shoulders and between the shoulder blades.   Not: neck and traps shrugging up.

Why this one, and not the face pull
The face pull needs something to attach to. This lesson doesn't — everything here anchors to your own body, so it works in a hotel room with nothing but a band in your bag. You'll learn the anchored face pull in Lesson 6.

Do thisStraight arms at chest height, hands about shoulder-width on the band. 15–25 reps.If that’s too difficultWiden your grip on the band — the further apart your hands start, the easier it is.If that’s too easyNarrow your grip, or hold the fully open position for 2 seconds each rep.

When you repeat this on Day 5

Do not add exercises. Pick one progression per exercise:

  • Use the next heavier band.
  • Shorten the band (step wider, or choke up on the ends).
  • Add a 2-second squeeze at the hard end.
  • Add a 3-second return.
  • Keep tension between reps — never go slack.
  • Add 2–5 reps.
  • Reduce transition time, only if form stays clean.
