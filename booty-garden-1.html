Booty Garden is a short course about growing your glutes.

Not “activating” them. Not making them burn for thirty seconds and hoping for the best. Growing them.

We're going to use familiar movement principles, serious resistance, and gradual increases to help you build the biggest glutes you can with the equipment available to you. No gimmicks. No magic exercise. Just a garden we intend to grow.

If you came from Turn Up the Life

Welcome back. You already know that movement is not random. You've learned how to create power from your hips, build tension, remove empty space, connect opposite sides of your body, use external anchors, and move continuously.

Those lessons don't end here — they're the soil. Turn Up the Life teaches your entire body. Booty Garden spends a little more time watering one flower bed. Nothing in the Academy changes. You're simply adding more fertilizer. 🌱

If you're new here

You're welcome in the garden too. Each Booty Garden lesson can be completed as a short, standalone session. We'll explain every setup, give you beginner-friendly cues, and show you what to try when a movement is too easy or too difficult. You don't need to know the Turn Up the Life system before you begin — you'll learn what you need as the garden grows.

What Is Fertilizer?

The 🌱 icon marks work that provides an especially useful growth stimulus for your glutes. That doesn't mean the exercise is magical — it means we're using it intentionally:

  • With enough resistance
  • Through a useful range
  • With control
  • Close enough to fatigue
  • With a plan to make it more demanding over time

Booty Garden is not a collection of random booty exercises. It's practice with a purpose.

Before you start

Meet the Garden

Your booty is made of three glute muscles — gluteus maximus, medius, and minimus. Consider those names planted as seeds; you don't need to memorize them yet. For today, think of them as a team with three important jobs.

  1. Creating force through the hips.
  2. Moving and controlling the leg out to the side.
  3. Keeping the pelvis steady while you move.

Today's exercises will ask your glutes to perform all three jobs. No exercise belongs to only one muscle — we're simply changing the task, and noticing where you feel the greatest demand.

Only have one band?

That's okay. Use today's test to learn what you own. If it's too light, you'll learn ways to increase the challenge before buying another. If it's too strong to use safely, you'll know your next purchase should be a lighter band.

Part one

Choose Your Mini Band

Mini Band Testworn above the knees

Place the mini band above your knees.

Lateral steps 10 each directionKickbacks 8 each side
  • Perform 10 lateral steps in each direction.
  • Then perform 8 standing kickbacks on each side.
  • Use a wall or another stable object for balance if needed.
Watch · Lateral walk form Watch · Standing kickback form
Keep this mini band if
  • You can complete every repetition with control.
  • You can feel and resist the band throughout the movement.
  • The final repetitions are noticeably harder.
  • You could perform only a few more good repetitions.
Try a lighter mini band if
  • You cannot take a complete lateral step.
  • You cannot move your leg backward without leaning.
  • The band pulls your knees inward.
  • You have to swing, twist, or rush.
  • The band restricts the movement more than it resists it.
Try a stronger mini band if
  • You barely need to press against it.
  • Your final repetitions feel like your first.
  • You could easily perform twice as much work.
  • The band becomes slack during the movement.
Keep any lighter or stronger bands nearby — the workout may change your answer.
Part two

Choose Your Long-Loop Band

Long-Loop Band Testover the shoulders, held near the chest

You already know how to squat. Today, the squat is helping you evaluate your band.

Squats 10 repsPace 3 seconds down
  • Stand on the middle of the long-loop band with both feet.
  • Bring the other end securely over your shoulders. Hold it near your chest so it doesn't pull against your neck.
  • Lower for three seconds.
  • Press the floor away and stand tall.

To remove it: bend your knees slightly to slacken the band, lift it off one shoulder at a time, then step out of the loop at your feet. Don't yank it straight up over your head under full tension.

Watch · Long-loop squat setup
Keep this long-loop band if
  • You can set it up and remove it safely.
  • You can use your normal controlled range.
  • The band becomes meaningfully harder as you stand.
  • The final repetitions slow down.
  • You could perform only two or three more good repetitions.
Try a lighter long-loop band if
  • You cannot set it up safely.
  • It pulls you out of position.
  • It prevents you from reaching your normal depth.
  • You have to bounce or rush to stand.
  • You cannot complete eight controlled repetitions.
Try a stronger long-loop band if
  • The band becomes slack.
  • Standing requires very little effort.
  • Repetition ten feels almost like repetition one.
  • You could easily perform many more repetitions.
This is your starting long-loop band — not your forever band, and not necessarily the correct band for every setup. You'll test it again when you learn your first long-loop thrust.
Your first Booty Garden

Prove the Choice

Now let's see whether those choices survive the garden. Set a timer for five minutes. Move continuously through the sequence below at a controlled pace. This is not a race — the goal is to notice what happens to your movement as your glutes begin to fatigue.

The Booty Garden Circuitrepeat continuously for five minutes

  1. 1. Long-loop squat — 8 reps. Lower with control. Press the floor away. Stand tall against the band.
    Sit toward the chair behind you. Then push the floor away.
  2. 2. Mini-band lateral walk — 8 steps right, 8 steps left. Keep space between your feet. Don't let the trailing leg snap inward.
    Your feet are on two train tracks. Don't let the tracks crash.
  3. 3. Mini-band standing kickback — 8 reps each side. Stay tall. Move from the hip without throwing your chest forward.
    Close the door behind you with your heel. Don't spill the drink on your head.

Return to the squat and repeat the sequence until the timer ends.

Together, these movements ask your garden to create force, work out to the side, and control the pelvis while one leg moves.

Wrap-up

The Final Band Check

Your bands passed if you could maintain the intended range, control every repetition, keep the band secure, feel the work becoming more difficult, and finish challenged without losing the movement.

Your band may be too light if

You completed the entire five minutes without slowing down, concentrating, or feeling meaningfully challenged. Try the next stronger band during your next Booty Garden lesson.

Your band may be too strong if

Your range became much smaller, your position changed, or you had to swing and rush to continue. Use the next lighter band.

Your bands may both be right without feeling equally difficult — they perform different jobs. You may even need different mini-band strengths for lateral walks and kickbacks. That's normal. For today, choose the mini band that lets you perform both movements well.

Before you go

Feed the Garden

Training gives your glutes a reason to grow. Food gives them the energy and building materials to do it.

Booty Garden assumes you're eating enough total food and enough protein to support muscle growth.

Can muscle grow while you're eating fewer calories than your body uses? Sometimes. It's more likely for some people than others, and careful nutrition and training can make it possible. But a prolonged calorie deficit generally makes gaining muscle slower and more difficult — the larger the deficit, the harder it is to give growth the energy it needs.

This is a glute-training course, not a nutrition program. We're not going to prescribe calories, calculate macros, or pretend one plan is right for every body. We're simply going to be honest:

If your main goal is to grow your glutes, consistently eating too little will work against that goal.

Protein matters because your body breaks it into amino acids and uses those to build and repair muscle. Your body can make some amino acids — it can't make the essential ones, so those have to come from the food you eat. They can come from animal foods, plant foods, or a combination. You don't need a particular shake, powder, or Booty Garden product.

Talk to a professional if

You're unsure whether you're eating enough, or increasing food requires medical, digestive, cultural, financial, or emotional considerations. A registered dietitian or another qualified healthcare professional can help in ways this course isn't built to.

For this course, we'll assume:

  • you're not intentionally maintaining a large calorie deficit
  • you regularly eat protein-containing foods
  • you're giving your body enough food and recovery to respond to training

Plant the work. Feed the garden. Give it time to grow.
